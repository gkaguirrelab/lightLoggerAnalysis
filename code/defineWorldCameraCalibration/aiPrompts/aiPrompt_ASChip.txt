I have a scientific instrument that includes an ASM7341 chip and an IMX219 camera chip.

The ASM7341 contains 8 narrow-band spectral sensors in the visible light range, and a 9th "clear" channel that is broad band. There is a 10th channel that is narrow band in the near infrared. The chip measures light intensity with 16 bit resolution. The chip is behind a cosine integrator and has a 2pi steradian field of view. I have available the tabular spectral sensitivities of each of the channels of the ASM7341 chip. I have obtained calibrated weights for each of the channels that relates the sensor counts to the integrated mean radiance measured by each channel. I am uncertain, however, if the weight is accurate for the 10th, IR channel as I am limited in my ability to produce and measure light in this range.

The IMX219 camera chip records and image subtends 120 x 90° of visual angle. I am able to express the output of the camera as the integrated radiance at each pixel in each of the three channels (R, G, B). I have available the tabular spectral sensitivities of the three channels of the IMX219 chip.

The fields of view of the ASM7341 and IMX219 are aligned and share the same center point.

I wish to reconstruct an estimate of the continuous, mean radiance spectrum as seen by the ASM7341. The routine "estimateRadianceSpectrumFromMinispect" does so using Tikhonov Regularization. I find that the estimated spectra are imperfect, in particular for outdoor settings.

I am interested in the possibility that I can use the mean, integrated radiance values for the R, G, and B channels from the simultaneously collected IMX219 image to constrain the reconstruction of the continuous radiance spectrum from the ASM7341 chip. I have a set of measurements that include:

- The estimated illuminant in a scene (in W/m2/sr/nm) in a scene, along with the "commonS" description of the wavelength support
- The integrated radiance values (in W/m2/sr) for the R, G, and B channels of the IMX219 camera, averaged over the entire image
- The channel values for the ASM7341 chip

These measurements are made for a range of light levels, indoors and outside.

Describe a strategy to 1) improve the estimate of the continuous, mean scene radiance spectrum by making use of the measurements from the IMX219 chip; 2) implement this approach in the measurements described above and use properties of the measurements to validate the reconstructed mean scene radiance spectrum.

I have attached relevant code samples and example mat files that contain the measured variables.
